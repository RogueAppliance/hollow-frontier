# Hollow Frontier — Phase specs

The original specs of phases 1-21, as they stood when each was done. An archive: what actually shipped, and why, is in DECISIONS.md.

1. ✅ **DONE (2026-10-26) — Scaffold.** MDK for 26.3, Java 25 toolchain, GeckoLib dep, mod id/package, registry classes (`ModItems`, `ModBlocks`, `ModEntities`, `ModBlockEntities`, `ModMenus`, `ModDataComponents`, `ModSounds`, `ModTabs`), datagen wiring (`GatherDataEvent` client/server), `genPlaceholders` task, empty `ASSETS.md`.

9. **The Hollow Earth** (split in three).
   - ✅ **DONE (2026-10-28) — 9a. The world itself.** Real noise settings with a thick crust ceiling (hanging spires, floating crust islands, waterfalls from the ceiling), four biomes of our own (Primordial Jungle, Crystal Steppes, Ember Badlands, Concavian Highlands: terrain, material rules, palette, fog/water colours, base vegetation), a multi-noise biome source, the sky-colour fix, the inner sun, time rules, the crossing reworked into a fall through the crust, and a safe `landing` below the ceiling. Spawn and biome-tag hooks for 10a/10b. *Threads:* Symmes' Hole names the gateway shaft. *Done when:* the dimension generates its own terrain and biomes, plain blue sky never shows, landing tests pass across many seeds, and runPhoto shows the crossing, each biome and the sun.
   - **9b. Life and detail** (split in three on 2026-10-28, one session each).
     - ✅ **DONE (2026-10-28) — 9b1. Flora and ambience.** Biome features that make each biome distinct: giant tree ferns and cycads (Jungle), glowing crystal outcrops (Steppes), smoking ash vents in fields of ash (Badlands), pale standing stones and toppled pillars (Highlands). (Glow-berry "glowworm stars" hanging from the crust were tried and cut: they can't be seen from the ground.) New blocks: Fern Frond, Ash, Ash Vent. Ambient sounds and particles per biome (crystal glints, cinder moths). All placed with the open-air scan. *Done when:* each biome reads as itself from the ground, the features place in tests, and the build and tests pass.
     - ✅ **DONE (2026-10-29) — 9b2. Small creatures.** Fern Skitter (Jungle: flocks of tiny skittish dinosaurs that snatch dropped food), Glassback Hare (Steppes: crystal growths on its back that glow), Ash Salamander (Badlands: fireproof, basks on vents, swims in lava) and Pale Raven (Highlands: perches on standing stones, flies off toward the nearest Kindled site once 9b3 adds them). GeckoLib models, spawns in our own biomes, vanilla drops only (new drops wait for 9c's uses). *Done when:* all four spawn and behave, with models and tests.
     - ✅ **DONE (2026-10-29) — 9b3. Lore sites.** Kindled traces in the Highlands (a waystone, a sealed door, murals), one small abandoned **Gray field lab** (restraint tables, specimen tanks, an ear-tagged hare) with a watching Gray probe that flees, and a **lost Symmes expedition camp** with a journal (vanilla written book) describing the crossing and something watching them. Structures must find the ground below the ceiling (see 9a's notes on heightmaps): a structure type of our own that reads the noise column. *Done when:* the three lore sites generate and have tests, and runPhoto shows them.
   - ✅ **DONE (2026-10-29) — 9c. Ores and gear.** Phlogiston crystal ore → phlogiston shard (glows, and already does something: light, a compass or steam upgrade, a trade); starsteel ore → raw/ingot starsteel; crusher dust and smelting. Starsteel tools and armor (place the tier against diamond and netherite, give it a perk, fit the look). Tier-3 Starsteel Horseshoes. Overworld seeds: Gazette rumours of airship lights, Symmes' Hole and the poles; the rare strange headstone on Boot Hill (cryptic epitaph, something odd beneath) if it fits the town blueprints cleanly. The **Hollow Earth advancement tab** (arrival, each biome, the ores, full starsteel, the horseshoes, the journal and lab as hidden finds) and filling the **Hollow Earth creative tab** (registered in 9a, holding only the crust blocks so far). *Done when:* every new item has a use now, the tab and advancements load, and the tests pass.

13. **The Grays and their craft** (split in four; 13c added 2026-10-29 on request).
    - ✅ **DONE (2026-10-29) — 13a. The Grays arrive.** Night flyovers and abductions (a beam that lifts mobs, or a player who isn't sneaking), Gray Wardens (ray pistol), and **crash sites**: rare, in the Overworld and the Hollow Earth, a downed saucer half-buried at the end of a scorched furrow, with salvage to pry out of it: **hull plating** (alien alloy), a **damaged gravity drive**, a **beam emitter**, a **lens array** and a **navigation core**. Not every wreck has every part, so a full set means finding several. *Done when:* flyovers, abductions, crash sites and Wardens work, the salvage drops, and tests cover them.
    - ✅ **DONE (2026-10-29) — 13b. Phlogiston tech.** Ray Pistol, Phlogiston Rifle (3D, like the frontier guns), **phlogiston cells** (charged from 9c's phlogiston shards). The Phlogiston Generator was dropped on request. The gravity drive is repaired here with phlogiston shards and starsteel. *Done when:* the weapons, cells and generator work with recipes and tests.
    - ✅ **DONE (2026-10-29) — 13c. The Prototype Saucer.** The player builds a craft of their own from the salvage, frontier-style: crafted whole from eight alien alloy plates round the repaired drive (a Saucer Cradle multiblock came first, and was replaced after the playtest as too fiddly), set down like the airship, with each part fitted by using it on the craft and shown on its GeckoLib model. It flies as a rideable entity: full 3D flight, hovering, about three times the airship's speed, burning phlogiston cells, a pilot and one passenger. Fittings: the **lens array** fires a heat laser at the ground (hurts mobs, scorches and sets fire to a small spot; heat builds up and has to cool; it leaves town blocks alone); the **beam emitter** is a tractor beam straight down that lifts and carries mobs, animals, items and players, then lowers or drops them (players can be beamed up unless the `hollowfrontierSaucerBeamsPlayers` gamerule is off, and a carried player struggles free by jumping); the **navigation core** adds a heading to the nearest polar gateway and a waypoint. Ideas for later fittings: a cloak (invisible at night), crop-circle stamping (a prank on the frontier the Gazette reports), a cargo hold. *Done when:* the saucer can be built from salvage, flies, fires, beams entities and players up, and the tests cover it.
    - ✅ **DONE (2026-10-29) — 13d. The Mothership.** The boss (a big saucer with drones), the Phlogiston Beacon that summons it, and the story's payoff (breaking the implants' leash on the men in dusters and the outlaws). It can be fought on foot with phlogiston weapons or in the air from the player's own saucer. *Done when:* the boss is beatable and the ending plays.
14. ✅ **DONE (2026-10-29) — Polish and release.** Optional JEI/Jade/Curios compat, the final advancement pass (the Frontier and Steam tabs exist since 2026-10-28; each phase adds its own tab), the final ASSETS.md pass, balance, and a release.
15. **Aftermath** (planned 2026-10-29 after the post-release audit; split in three, one session each). Breaking the leash (13d) shuts down most of the world's events: no abductions or men in black, and only a third of the wild outlaws. A Mothership rematch is the same fight with the same salvage. This phase gives the world new things to do once the story is over. Everything here starts only once `Leash.isBroken`, so it works in worlds that already finished 13d. Each sub-phase adds game tests, lang, CHANGELOG, ASSETS.md entries and runPhoto shots for anything visual.
    - ✅ **DONE (2026-10-30) — 15a. The holdouts (the Grays fall back).** In place of the abductions, every few nights a **Gray Landing** comes down near a player in the Overworld or the Hollow Earth. It is a small saucer set down in a scorched clearing, with Gray Wardens, a few drones and a salvage cache, and it takes off at dawn with whatever is left. Clearing it before dawn gives salvage, phlogiston cells and now and then a damaged fitting. It reuses the 13a crash-site parts and the existing mobs, and gets a config block in `hollowfrontier-ufo-server.toml` (enabled, interval, chance) plus `/hollowfrontier ufo landing` for ops. The Gazette reports landings, and townsfolk talk about them. *Done when:* landings come down, fight, take off at dawn and pay out, with tests for the spawn, the dawn take-off and the loot.
    - ✅ **DONE (2026-10-30) — 15b. The Mothership's return.** Each Mothership called after the first is a step harder. A per-world counter, saved with `Leash`, adds health, a drone each wave and a faster death ray, up to a cap. Rematches also drop the saucer fittings planned in 13c, which so far nothing gives: the **Cloak** (the saucer can't be seen at night, and mobs lose track of it) and the **Cargo Hold** (a 9-slot inventory you open from the saucer, kept when it's folded). Each Mothership drops one of them, so it takes two rematches to get both. This sub-phase also gives the saucer an **owner**: only the player who set it down (or their team) can fold it or take its fittings (AUDIT.md finding 4). New advancements: Encore and Fully Fitted. *Done when:* rematches scale and drop the fittings, the fittings work and survive folding, the owner lock holds, and tests cover all three.
    - ✅ **DONE (2026-10-30) — 15c. What the frontier makes of itself.**
      - **Freed men:** an outlaw freed by the leash isn't just a wanderer any more. Talk to him in a town and, for a gold coin, he signs on as a Deputy for that town (he becomes a Lawman and stands with the town in raids), or he goes home as a Prospector townsperson.
      - **Restoring Concavia:** a Kindled ally can bring phlogiston shards and starsteel to a Crystal Ruins' fountain to restore it, in three stages. Each stage swaps in a less broken blueprint piece (a tower raised, the sanctum lit) and unlocks Kindled trades.
      - **The waystone network:** at the last stage the ruin's waystone wakes. Woken waystones link to one another and to opened Polar Gateways, so a player can travel between them for a phlogiston shard. This is the mod's first fast travel, and the endgame's sink for phlogiston and starsteel.
      - The Codex gets a closing chapter, "After", and the Gazette runs stories of the aftermath.
      - *Done when:* freed outlaws can be hired or sent home, a ruin restores in stages, linked waystones carry players between ruins and gateways, and tests cover each step.
16. ✅ **DONE (2026-10-30) — The bank** (planned 2026-10-29; one session). Coins today buy only drinks, trades and fines. A deposit box gives players a reason to save, and something to lose when a raid gets the vault.
    - **Safe deposit boxes:** sneak-use a town's Banker to open your box in that town's bank. It's an 18-slot chest menu, private to you, shared by nobody. The first opening costs a gold coin; the box is free after that. A Banker won't serve anyone with two stars or more, the same as other townsfolk. Going through the Banker, rather than a new block, means towns that already exist get banks without being regenerated. Boxes are saved in a new Overworld `SavedData` (`TownBanks`), keyed by the town's structure key, then by player UUID. Each box is a live shared view written straight back to the saved data, the same pattern as `SaddlebagContainer`, so two windows on one box can't duplicate items.
    - **Interest:** coins in a box earn about 3% a day: 1 copper coin per full 32 copper's worth, with a gold coin counting as 9. It's capped at 8 copper a day per box. Interest is worked out when you open the box, from the days since your last visit, up to 7 days, so nothing ticks for boxes nobody opens.
    - **Raids:** when a gang cracks the vault, it also takes a share of the coins in every box of that town. The share is a new `depositsAtRisk` setting, default 0.25; 0 turns this off. That includes boxes of players who are offline. Kill the bagman and every coin goes back to the box it came from. Each owner is told on their next visit or login what was taken, from which town, and whether it came back. The Gazette's raid report says how much the bank lost.
    - **Advancements:** Money in the Bank (open a box), Compound Interest (draw a day's interest), Saved the Savings (kill the bagman before he gets your coins out).
    - *Done when:* boxes open, store, persist and can't duplicate items; interest pays out and caps; a cracked vault takes the box share and a dead bagman returns it; tests cover all of it.
17. ✅ **DONE (2026-10-29) — The card table** (planned 2026-10-29; one session). Blackjack in the saloon: the player against the house. It works the same on a server as in singleplayer.
    - **Card Table:** a saloon furniture block, a green felt top on a wooden table. It's craftable, and new saloons (standalone and in towns) get one. A table deals only when a Barmaid is within 8 blocks, because she's the house; with nobody there, it says "Nobody's dealing." At **Josie's** table (she's the card sharp) the dealer hits on soft 17 and the limit is doubled: worse odds, bigger pots.
    - **The game:** a menu run by the server, so a modified client can't see or change the deck. A fresh 52-card deck is shuffled for every hand. The dealer stands on 17. A natural blackjack pays 3:2, rounded down. You can double down on your first two cards. Splitting and insurance are left out of this version. You bet 1–18 copper coins (1–36 at Josie's table), and the coins are taken from your inventory when the cards are dealt. Winnings go to your inventory, or drop at your feet if it's full. Closing the menu mid-hand stands for you and settles the hand, so walking away can't dodge a loss. Players with Tipsy II or more may bet double.
    - **The screen:** your hand and the dealer's, with the hole card face down until the dealer plays, plus bet, Deal, Hit, Stand and Double buttons. The buttons go through vanilla's `clickMenuButton`, so no new network payload is needed. The art is a card-face atlas and a card back drawn by `TextureArt`, with the ranks and suits drawn in code. There are sounds for the shuffle, a card dealt and a coin.
    - **Advancements:** Twenty-One (a natural blackjack), Five-Card Charlie (win with five cards), Cleaned Out (lose five hands in a row).
    - **Later, reusing the same table, cards and screen:** five-card draw poker against townsfolk.
    - *Done when:* the table deals only with a barmaid near; hand values with soft and hard aces, the dealer's play and every payout are right (tested with stacked decks); coins move correctly; leaving mid-hand settles the hand; tests cover all of it.
18. ✅ **DONE (2026-10-29) — The saloon bounty board** (planned 2026-10-29; one session). Today the Bounty Board makes up a new outlaw each time it's used, and nothing on it shows. In this phase it becomes a board of posters you can read at a glance.
    - **Posted bounties:** the board gets a block entity holding up to six posted bounties (`BountyData`), the same for everyone who looks. A board in a town shares its list with the other boards in that town, kept with that town's record. Posters that have been collected or have run out are replaced each dawn.
    - **What it shows** (a `BlockEntityRenderer` drawing text the way signs do, so it updates live and follows the game's language):
      - Each poster has the outlaw's name, the reward in gold, the compass direction and distance from town, and a sketched face picked from a few variants.
      - A poster someone has taken shows "TAKEN" and their name.
      - A badge turned in gets a red "DEAD" stamp until the next dawn.
      - A raid leader who got away goes up as a bigger poster at the top.
      - A player with two stars or more gets their own poster, showing their name and fine. Paying the fine at the board takes it down.
    - **Using it:** use the poster you're looking at (worked out from where the hit lands on the board's face) to take that bounty. Turning a badge in and paying fines work as they do now.
    - **Placement and existing worlds:** saloons (standalone and in towns) get a board on the wall. Boards already placed in existing worlds have to gain the block entity, either created when the chunk loads or when the board is first used. Check which of these actually happens before relying on it. Until a board has its block entity, it keeps working the old way.
    - **Advancements:** none new. The existing bounty advancements just need to keep working.
    - *Done when:* boards post, show, update and refresh bounties; taking, turning in and paying fines change what's shown; boards in one town agree; old boards keep working; runPhoto shows a full board; tests cover the list, the shared town list and the dawn refresh.
19. ✅ **DONE (2026-10-30) — Voices** (planned 2026-10-29; one session, or two if the line count runs long). Townsfolk are nearly silent: besides refusing trade, their only lines are 9 grumbles about the 11a mysteries. Barmaids have 16 lines, lawmen 9 and outlaws 10. This phase makes the frontier talk, and makes what it says follow what's going on.
    - **One chatter system:** `Chatter`, used by every speaking NPC in place of each class picking its own lines. A line is chosen from a speaker's pool by **conditions**:
      - time of day and weather;
      - the player's wanted stars and suspicion;
      - whether this town was robbed, or is in a raid right now;
      - a bounty poster the player is carrying;
      - the gear the player is wearing (the cowboy outfit, the Starsteel Crown, a Kindled Sigil);
      - how far the story has got, in **acts**: the frontier → a gateway opened → the Grays → the leash broken.

      Each speaker has a cooldown, and so does each player, so nobody repeats a line or floods the chat. Lines still arrive as `<Name> line` in chat, as they do now. Lang comes through datagen as usual.
    - **When NPCs speak:**
      - a greeting the first time you come near in a while;
      - a line when you open a townsperson's trade screen;
      - **overheard talk**: now and then two townsfolk near you trade two or three lines of gossip, on the same conditions;
      - **combat barks**: outlaws when they spot you, when they reload, when they're hurt badly and when they run; lawmen when they make an arrest, when a posse rides and when the town is raided.
    - **How much:** about 6 general lines for each profession and lawman rank, plus situational lines (robbed, raid, wanted, each act). Outlaws and bosses get barks and taunts. A few new Kindled lines and man-in-black lines for the later acts. Around 150 lines in all, in the voice of the existing barmaid and grumble lines.
    - *Done when:* every speaking NPC goes through `Chatter`; the conditions and cooldowns work (tested by forcing each condition); overheard talk happens; runPhoto isn't needed; the line count is in the CHANGELOG.
20. ✅ **DONE (2026-10-31) — The captives** (planned 2026-10-29; one session). Where the implanted outlaws come from: deep in the Hollow Earth, the Grays hold men taken from the frontier and fit them with implants. These are the people behind the night abductions (13a) and the "drifters" the leash (13d) drove.
    - **Gray holding pens:** a new Hollow Earth structure, rarer than a field station and placed with the same scan for open ground. It has a cell block with two to four **captives** (outlaws, now and then a townsperson or a lawman, all reusing the existing mobs in a new "held" state, like `isJailed`), an operating room with a restraint table under an implant rig, Gray Wardens on guard, and Sentinels outside.
    - **The procedure:** when a player is spotted, or as they come in, the Grays start on the captive strapped to the table. A "Procedure" boss bar fills over about 60 seconds. Kill the Wardens working the rig, or break the rig, to stop it. If it finishes, the captive gets up implanted and hostile. Once the leash is broken there's no procedure (nothing to implant into); the captives are just held, and the pens become another kind of holdout for 15a.
    - **Freeing them:** open a cell and talk to the captive.
      - A freed captive tells their story, in new `Chatter` lines from phase 19: taken in the night (Missing Time), the men in black, a cold table. They hand you a **Letter Home** naming their town. Bring it to that town's Bounty Board or sheriff for a reward and a little goodwill (heat off, if you have any). The captive then walks off, the same way freed outlaws do now.
      - A captive outlaw who's on a Wanted Poster you're carrying can be **brought in alive**: he goes with you as a jailed prisoner, and once he's at that town's jail the poster pays in full. This links to phase 18's board, where the poster gets a "CAPTURED" stamp.
    - **Advancements:** Jailbreak (free a captive), Not on My Watch (stop a procedure), Special Delivery (deliver a Letter Home), Dead or Alive (turn in a rescued outlaw alive).
    - *Done when:* pens generate in the Hollow Earth with captives; the procedure runs, can be stopped and finishes into a hostile; freed captives speak and hand over letters; letters and live turn-ins pay; behaviour after the leash is right; tests cover all of it.
21. ✅ **DONE (2026-10-31) — Frontier humour** (planned 2026-10-30; split in three, one session each). The story is a dark one (implants, abductions, a cold table), and the frontier has a dry voice already ("Some of 'em even sober"). This phase makes that voice the counterweight: frontier folk who treat a cosmic conspiracy as one more nuisance, like the weather. The humour is deadpan and comes from the world: no memes, no fourth-wall jokes, no real people. Every new line goes through lang (datagen), so it can be translated, and every joke has to work for a player who has never heard it before. Each sub-phase adds lang, CHANGELOG and game tests.
    - ✅ **DONE (2026-10-30) — 21a. Words** (text, plus the little code that picks it).
      - **Gazette back page:** a new last page, picked at random each issue from three kinds. **Classifieds** ("FOR SALE: saucer part, still humming, no questions"), **letters to the editor** (cranks, a widow fed up with the airships, a rancher who wants his cow back) and **corrections** ("Last week's paper said the mayor was eaten by a mammoth. He was only sat on."). About 10 of each. Some only print in a later act (Chatter's acts) or after a recent mystery (`MysteryLog`), so the jokes follow the story.
      - **Gray field notes:** a written book ("Field Notes, Specimen Group: Cowpokes") in the caches of field labs (10b), Gray Landings (15a) and now and then a crash site. Each copy has 3-4 entries picked from about 16: clinical notes on frontier life by someone who doesn't get it. ("Subject drinks fermented grain, then challenges a cactus to a duel. The cactus wins.") Each loot table rolls it through a new loot function, so copies differ.
      - **Boot Hill epitaphs:** some of Boot Hill's crosses get a sign with a joke epitaph, picked from about 12 original ones ("He called the Barmaid's coffee weak"). They're written through lang like the strange grave's sign, and the strange grave stays the only serious one. Check whether the structure can choose a different set for each town when it's placed; if it can't, bake a fixed set into each blueprint variant.
      - **Men in black cover stories:** new Chatter lines for men in black (act 3) when the player asks about a recent sign, using the MUTILATIONS / CROP_CIRCLES / SIGHTINGS / LANDINGS facts. Deadpan 1890s excuses that get worse each time: "That was Venus." "Swamp gas." "Venus again." Plus a Gazette correction that "Agents of the Bureau" have asked to print.
      - **Reload barks:** outlaws get Chatter barks for the slow muzzleloader reload, from both sides: an outlaw asking for time while he reloads, and taunting a player who's reloading. They use the reload bark trigger from phase 19.
      - *Done when:* each kind of line shows up in the right conditions (tested by forcing them), the field notes roll in their loot with different entries, epitaph signs are on Boot Hill, and the line count is in the CHANGELOG.
    - ✅ **DONE (2026-10-31) — 21b. Tipsy and the saloon** (small mechanics on what exists).
      - **Slurred talk:** at Tipsy II and up, NPC lines and your own Gazette come out a little slurred (a letter doubled or swapped here and there, worked out client-side from the translated text, so it works in every language). A hiccup sound now and then.
      - **Moonshine sightings:** at Tipsy III, moonshine sometimes shows the drinker a saucer that isn't there: a client-only copy of the 11a airship sighting, seen only by them, never logged, never in the Gazette. If they mention it, nobody saw it. Barmaids get a line about it.
